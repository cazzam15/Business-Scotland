📋 SQA Key Areas: Role of business in society · Types of organisations · Objectives · PESTEC · Stakeholders · Enterprise
Section A — Business Organisations & Liability
Q1 · Multiple Choice
Which type of business organisation has LIMITED LIABILITY and sells shares PRIVATELY (not on the stock exchange)?
ASole trader
BPartnership
CPrivate limited company (Ltd)
DSocial enterprise
✓ C — Private limited company (Ltd). An Ltd has limited liability (shareholders only risk their investment) and shares are sold privately among known individuals. A PLC sells shares publicly on the stock exchange.
Q2 · Short Answer (2 marks)
Explain what is meant by "unlimited liability" and state which type of business organisation this applies to.
✓ Model answer:
Unlimited liability means the owner is personally responsible for ALL business debts, including using personal assets (house, car, savings) to pay them if the business cannot. (1)
It applies to sole traders and most partnerships. (1)

Q4 · Multiple Choice
A new competitor enters the market selling similar products at lower prices. Which type of external factor is this?
APolitical factor
BEconomic factor
CSocial factor
DCompetitive factor
✓ D — Competitive factor. Changes in competition (new entrants, rivals' pricing, new products from competitors) are competitive external factors. The business has no control over these but must respond to them.
Q5 · Short Answer (3 marks)
Identify THREE different stakeholders of a large supermarket and state ONE interest each has in the business.
✓ Model answer (1 mark each):
• Shareholders/owners: interested in profit and dividends (return on investment). (1)
• Employees: interested in fair pay, job security, and good working conditions. (1)
• Customers: interested in good quality products at fair prices and excellent service. (1)
Other valid answers: suppliers (payment on time), local community (jobs, local impact), government (tax revenue, legal compliance), banks (loan repayments).
📋 SQA Key Areas: Market research · Marketing mix (4 Ps) · Product life cycle · Pricing strategies · Promotion · Place/distribution
Section A — Marketing Mix & Research
Q1 · Fill in the Blanks — 4 Ps
Complete the marketing mix:
The four elements of the marketing mix are:
· · ·
✓ The 4 Ps: Product · Price · Place · Promotion. All four must be coordinated to create an effective marketing strategy that satisfies customer needs.
Q2 · Multiple Choice
A new smartphone is launched at £999. Six months later the price drops to £799. Which pricing strategy is being used?
APenetration pricing
BPrice skimming
CCompetitive pricing
DCost-plus pricing
✓ B — Price skimming. Skimming: high initial price to "skim" profits from customers willing to pay a premium (early adopters), then price drops to attract more customers. Common with new technology.
Q3 · Short Answer (4 marks)
Describe the FIVE stages of the Product Life Cycle and state what happens to sales at each stage.
✓ Model answer:
1. R&D: product developed and tested — no sales yet, high costs. (1)
2. Introduction: product launched — low sales, high promotion costs, may make a loss. (1)
3. Growth: sales rise rapidly — profits begin, competitors may enter. (1)
4. Maturity: sales peak — high competition, extension strategies used. (1)
5. Decline: sales fall — product may be updated, replaced, or discontinued.
Q4 · Multiple Choice
Which type of market research collects NEW data directly from customers using questionnaires and interviews?
ADesk (secondary) research
BField (primary) research
CAnalysis of competitor sales data
DReading government statistics
✓ B — Field (primary) research. Field research collects brand new data: surveys, questionnaires, interviews, focus groups, observation. It's specific to your needs but more expensive and time-consuming than desk research.
📋 SQA Key Areas: Types of costs · Break-even · Cash budgeting · Income statement · Sources of finance
Section A — Break-Even & Costs
Q1 · Multiple Choice
A business has fixed costs of £20,000. Each product sells for £50 and costs £30 to make. What is the break-even point?
A400 units
B667 units
C1,000 units
D2,000 units
✓ C — 1,000 units.
Contribution per unit = Selling Price − Variable Cost = £50 − £30 = £20
Break-even = Fixed Costs ÷ Contribution = £20,000 ÷ £20 = 1,000 units
Q2 · Fill in the Blanks — Income Statement
Complete the income statement structure:

− Cost of Sales
= Profit
− (overheads)
= Net (or Loss) for the year
✓ Sales Revenue − Cost of Sales = Gross Profit − Expenses = Net Profit (or Loss)
Q3 · Multiple Choice
Which of the following is an INTERNAL source of finance?
ABank loan
BShare issue
CRetained profit
DMortgage
✓ C — Retained profit. Internal sources come from within the business: retained profit (kept rather than paid to owners), selling assets, owner's savings. No interest to pay — a key advantage over external sources.
Q4 · Short Answer (3 marks)
Explain the difference between FIXED costs and VARIABLE costs. Give ONE example of each.
✓ Model answer:
Fixed costs remain the same regardless of how much is produced. Example: rent, insurance, management salaries. (1+1)
Variable costs change directly in proportion to the level of output — more production = higher variable costs. Example: raw materials, packaging, direct labour per item. (1+1)

🔬 Key Facts & Equations

Essential knowledge for the SQA exam — learn these!

🔬 Cell Biology

Aerobic Respiration
Glucose + Oxygen → CO₂ + Water + ATP
Occurs in: cytoplasm (glycolysis) + mitochondria
Anaerobic — Yeast
Glucose → Ethanol + CO₂ (+ 2 ATP)
Anaerobic — Muscle
Glucose → Lactic acid (+ 2 ATP)
DNA Base Pairing
A — T   |   G — C
Double helix held by complementary base pairs
Osmosis
Water moves: HIGH water conc. → LOW water conc.
Through a selectively permeable membrane (passive)
Protein Synthesis
DNA → mRNA (transcription, in nucleus)
mRNA → Protein (translation, at ribosome)
Genetic Engineering (5 steps)
1. Identify gene → 2. Extract gene (restriction enzymes) → 3. Extract plasmid → 4. Insert gene into plasmid (ligase) → 5. Insert plasmid into host bacterium

🦠 Multicellular Organisms

Reflex Arc
Receptor → Sensory neuron → Relay neuron → Motor neuron → Effector
Blood Glucose Regulation
High glucose → Pancreas releases Insulin → Liver converts glucose→glycogen
Low glucose → Pancreas releases Glucagon → Liver converts glycogen→glucose
Brain Regions
Cerebrum: thought/memory  |  Cerebellum: balance/coordination
Medulla: heart rate/breathing (unconscious)
Genetics Terms
Dominant (capital) = expressed with 1 copy
Recessive (lowercase) = needs 2 copies (homozygous)
Heterozygous = Aa  |  Homozygous = AA or aa
Plant Transport
Xylem: water + minerals UPWARD (dead cells)
Phloem: sugars UP and DOWN (living cells)
Transpiration Rate ↑ when…
↑ Wind speed  |  ↓ Humidity  |  ↑ Temperature  |  ↑ Light intensity

🌍 Life on Earth

Photosynthesis
CO₂ + H₂O → Glucose + O₂ (using light energy, in chloroplasts)
Limiting factors: light intensity, CO₂ concentration, temperature
Energy Transfer in Food Chains
Only ~10% of energy transfers between trophic levels
~90% lost as heat (respiration, movement, waste)
Nitrogen Cycle
Nitrogen-fixing bacteria: N₂ → nitrates (usable)
Decomposers: dead matter → ammonium
Denitrifying bacteria: nitrates → N₂ (back to atmosphere)
Natural Selection
Variation → Selection pressure → Survival of fittest → Reproduction → Allele frequency changes over generations
Ecological Relationships
Predation: predator benefits, prey harmed
Mutualism: both benefit (e.g. Rhizobium + legumes)
Parasitism: parasite benefits, host harmed
Competition: intraspecific (same sp.) / interspecific (diff. sp.)

📚 Key Facts & Key Terms

Essential knowledge for the SQA exam — learn these!

🏢 Understanding Business

Sectors of Industry
Primary: extracting raw materials (farming, mining)
Secondary: manufacturing/making products
Tertiary: providing services (retail, banking)
Sectors of Economy
Private sector: profit-driven, privately owned
Public sector: government-owned, provides services
Third sector: charities, social enterprises, voluntary orgs
Types of Business Organisation
Sole trader: 1 owner, unlimited liability, keeps all profit
Partnership: 2–20 owners, shared profit/loss, usually unlimited liability
Private Ltd (Ltd): limited liability, shares sold privately
Public Ltd (PLC): listed on stock exchange, limited liability
PESTEC External Factors
Political · Environmental · Social · Technological · Economic · Competitive
Stakeholders
Owners, employees, customers, suppliers, banks, local community, government
Business Objectives
Profit · Market share · Customer satisfaction · Social responsibility · Survival · Innovation

📣 Management of Marketing

Marketing Mix — 4 Ps
Product · Price · Place · Promotion
Product Life Cycle
R&D → Introduction → Growth → Maturity → Decline
Extension strategies used at Maturity to delay Decline
Market Research Types
Field (primary): new data — surveys, interviews, focus groups
Desk (secondary): existing data — internet, sales records, reports
Pricing Strategies
Price skimming: high then reduce  |  Penetration: low to gain share
Competitive: match rivals  |  Cost-plus: costs + % markup
Premium: high price for luxury/status
Promotion Types
Above-the-line: TV, radio, national press (mass audience)
Below-the-line: direct mail, social media, loyalty schemes (targeted)

⚙️ Management of Operations

Methods of Production
Job: one unique product at a time (bespoke, high quality, expensive)
Batch: groups of similar products made together
Flow (mass): continuous production of identical products (efficient, low unit cost)
Quality
Quality control: inspect at END of process
Quality assurance: check THROUGHOUT process (proactive)
Quality inputs: use high-quality raw materials from the start
Inventory Management
Overstocking: wasted cash, storage costs, risk of obsolescence
Understocking: lost sales, disappointed customers, halted production

👥 Management of People

Recruitment Documents
Job description: duties, responsibilities, pay
Person specification: qualifications, skills, experience required
Types of Training
Induction: for new employees — policies, H&S, role
On-the-job: at workplace, by experienced colleague
Off-the-job: at college/training centre, specialist
Maslow's Hierarchy of Needs
(1) Physiological (pay) → (2) Safety (job security) → (3) Social (teamwork) → (4) Esteem (recognition) → (5) Self-actualisation (full potential)
Payment Systems
Salary: fixed annual pay (monthly)  |  Wages: per hour
Commission: % of sales  |  Piece rate: per item produced
Bonus: extra payment for performance
Key Legislation
Equality Act 2010: protects against discrimination (age, gender, race, disability, religion etc.)
Health & Safety at Work Act 1974: safe working environment

💰 Management of Finance

Break-Even Formula
Break-even (units) = Fixed Costs ÷ (Selling Price − Variable Cost per unit)
Contribution per unit = Selling Price − Variable Cost per unit
Margin of Safety = Actual Sales − Break-even Sales
Income Statement Structure
Sales Revenue
− Cost of Sales
= Gross Profit
− Expenses (overheads)
= Net Profit (or Loss) for the year
Types of Costs
Fixed costs: unchanged by output (rent, insurance, management salaries)
Variable costs: change with output (raw materials, packaging, direct labour)
Sources of Finance
Internal: retained profit, owner's savings, selling assets
External: bank loan, overdraft, mortgage, share issue, venture capital, grants
Cash Flow Issues & Solutions
Problem: more cash going out than coming in (negative cash flow)
Solutions: arrange overdraft, chase debtors, delay payments, cut costs, increase sales
