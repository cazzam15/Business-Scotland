The course assessment

Question paper 78%
70 marks · 2 hours 15 minutes · calculator allowed
Section 1 — 30 marks
One multi-part question (parts worth 1–8 marks) on a case study of a real organisation, with text, financial data, graphs and charts. Spend about 15 minutes reading it.
Section 2 — 40 marks
Five questions worth 8 marks each, from any of the five areas of study. Tests breadth of knowledge.
Assignment 22%
30 marks, scaled to 20 · business report on a topic you choose · 2,000 words + max 4 pages of appendices
Assignment marks
Introduction 2 · Research 4 · Analysis & interpretation 13 · Conclusions & recommendations 10 · Structure 1
What changed in 2026–27
Paper cut from 90 to 70 marks and from 2 h 45 min to 2 h 15 min. Section 2 is now five 8-mark questions. Course content is unchanged.

Command words

Describe
Relevant factual points. 1 mark per point; a second mark for a point you develop.
Explain
Cause and effect. Every mark needs a point of identification and a point of explanation: "X — which means Y".
Compare
1 mark per similarity or difference. Write both sides in one sentence with "whereas".
Discuss
Points on both sides (e.g. costs and benefits). Marks for each point and each development. Full marks usually need both sides.
Justify
Reasons why something is a good choice. Each valid justification, and development, earns a mark.
Distinguish
A clear difference between two things: "centralised … whereas decentralised …".
Define
The meaning of a term. 1 mark each.
Suggest
A sensible solution or idea, e.g. ways to improve a ratio.

The general marking principles in the specimen paper define describe, explain and compare. The others follow the wording used in recent marking instructions ("valid discussion point", "valid justification", "valid distinction", "valid definition").

Current ratio
Current assets : current liabilities
Ability to pay short-term debts · about 2:1 is often seen as ideal
Acid-test ratio
(Current assets − inventory) : current liabilities
Stricter liquidity test · about 1:1 is often seen as ideal
Rate of inventory turnover
Cost of sales ÷ average inventory
Average inventory = (opening + closing) ÷ 2

Financial statements & cash budgets interpret — preparation not required

Income Statement
Sales revenue − cost of sales = gross profit
Gross profit − expenses = profit for the year
Cost of sales
Opening inventory + purchases − closing inventory
Statement of Financial Position
Non-current assets + current assets − current liabilities − non-current liabilities = equity
Cash budget
Opening balance + receipts − payments = closing balance
This month's closing balance = next month's opening balance

Exam tips

  • Answer the command word. "Explain" needs a reason or consequence for every mark — not just a list.
  • Develop your points. A developed describe or discuss point can earn a second mark.
  • Use the case study. If a question says "used by" the organisation, take your examples from the text.
  • Cover both sides in discuss questions — full marks usually need at least one advantage and one disadvantage.
  • Interpret, don't just copy, numbers. Say what a change in a ratio means and why it might have happened.
  • Watch the clock. About 2 minutes per mark; don't spend 20 minutes on a 2-mark question.
  • Avoid repetition. Markers do not award the same point twice.
